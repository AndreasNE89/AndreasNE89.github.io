Repbook Iron Plan Privacy Policy

Effective 7 October 2026. Last updated 7 October 2026.

This policy covers Repbook Iron Plan on iPhone and Apple Watch (from the App Store) and on Android phones (from Google Play, where the app is labelled “Repbook” on the home screen). Where the apps differ, it says so.

In short: I don’t collect any of your data. Repbook Iron Plan has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no server. Your routines and workouts are stored on your own devices. They leave them only when you export a file yourself, or when you move to a new phone with your device’s own backup or transfer tools. If you buy Repbook Pro on Android, Google Play handles the payment.

Who is responsible

Repbook Iron Plan is made by Andreas Nelvik Engebretsen, an independent developer in Norway (“I”, “me”). For the personal data I do receive, which is only what you send me yourself (for example a support email) and the sales records Google Play gives me when you buy Repbook Pro (see Purchases), I am the data controller under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which applies in Norway through the EEA Agreement and the Norwegian Personal Data Act. On Google Play the app is published by my developer account SlowLorris89.

You can reach me at andreas.nelvik.engebretsen@gmail.com.

What the app stores on your device

Repbook Iron Plan stores what you create and choose in the app, in the app’s private storage on your iPhone, Apple Watch or Android phone:

  • Routines: their names and exercises, with target sets, reps, weight and rest time.
  • The built-in exercise library and the custom exercises you add, with their muscle groups.
  • Workouts: when each one started and finished, and each set’s weight, reps, the time you logged it and the rest before it.
  • Settings: kilograms or pounds, appearance, default rest time and whether you have seen the welcome guide.
  • On Android also: whether the one-time Repbook Pro offer has been shown, and the last answer from Google Play about whether you own Repbook Pro, so Pro features work as soon as the app opens.

Your workouts are information about your exercise. The app does not send any of it to me or to anyone else.

Data collection

I don’t collect any data through the app. It has no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDKs, does not track you across apps or websites, does not use the Android advertising ID, and has no account system or server. Its App Store privacy label is “Data Not Collected”, and its Google Play Data safety section says that no data is collected or shared.

iPhone and Apple Watch: the app makes no internet connections.

Android: Repbook’s own code makes no internet connections. The app includes Google’s Play Billing Library so that Repbook Pro can be bought through Google Play. The library works through the Google Play Store app on your phone, and it declares internet access so that it can send Google technical information about how billing works on your device. That information goes to Google, not to me, and Google handles it under the Google Privacy Policy.

The App Store and Google Play install and update the app. The app sends them nothing you enter.

iPhone and Apple Watch: If you have chosen in the Settings app to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass crash reports and usage statistics about the app on to me. They do not identify you, and you can turn this off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

Android: If you have allowed your phone to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, Google Play may show me statistics about how stable the app is, such as crashes and times it stopped responding (Google calls this Android vitals). Google shows them only when there is enough data to keep the reports anonymous, and they do not identify you. You can turn this off in your phone’s Google settings (on many phones, Settings › Google › Usage & diagnostics).

Apple Watch sync

If you use the Apple Watch app, your routines, workout logs, appearance, weight unit and set guidance sync between your iPhone and its paired Apple Watch through Apple’s built-in connection between the two. They go between your own devices only, never to me. The Android app has no watch app.

Backups and exported files

You can export a backup file from Settings. It holds your routines, exercises, workouts, sets and settings. On iPhone you save it with the Files app, on Android with the system file picker. Either way you choose where it goes: your device’s own storage, or a cloud drive such as iCloud Drive or Google Drive, which then keeps the file under its own terms. A backup made on iPhone restores on Android, and the other way round.

On Android, Repbook Pro can also export every logged set as a spreadsheet (CSV) file, saved the same way.

Backup and spreadsheet files hold your whole training log, so keep them somewhere you trust. The app never sends them anywhere by itself.

Moving to a new phone

Android: Repbook turns off Google’s cloud backup, so its data is never copied to your Google account. When you set up a new phone with Android 12 or later and choose to copy apps and data straight from your old phone (by cable or wirelessly), your training log and settings may move with the app. They go from your old phone to the new one, not to me. On Android 8 to 11 nothing is copied this way. To keep a copy or move your training log, export a backup file.

iPhone: If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, the app’s data is part of that device backup, which Apple or your computer keeps and restores under Apple’s terms.

Purchases

Android: Repbook Pro is a one-time in-app purchase, sold through Google Play’s billing system. Google takes the payment under its own terms and the Google Privacy Policy. The app never sees your payment details or your Google account. From Google Play on your phone it learns only whether your Google account owns Repbook Pro or has a purchase waiting for approval, and it confirms a new purchase to Google Play, as Google requires. It keeps the answer on your phone as a simple yes or no. Restore purchase asks Google Play again. The app sends me nothing about your purchase.

Google Play gives me a record of each sale for accounting, tax and refunds: the order number, product, date, price, taxes, status and the buyer’s country (in some countries also the region and postal code). I never receive payment card details. If you contact me about a purchase, I can look up your order by its order number or the email address of your Google account. I keep sales records because Norwegian bookkeeping law requires it (GDPR Art. 6(1)(c)), normally for five years.

iPhone and Apple Watch: the app has no in-app purchases. A Repbook Pro purchase on Google Play unlocks Pro on Android only.

Keeping your data safe

The app keeps its data in its private storage, which other apps cannot read, protected by your device’s screen lock and encryption.

How long data is kept and how to delete it

Your data stays on your device until you delete it. You can delete workouts, sets and routines in the app. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on that device; on Android, so does clearing the app’s storage (Settings › Apps › Repbook › Storage › Clear storage). Files you exported are not removed: delete them where you saved them. On iPhone, a copy can remain in earlier iCloud or computer backups of the device until those are replaced or deleted.

If you email me

If you write to me for support, I receive your email address and what you send, and use it only to answer you, which is my legitimate interest in helping you. I keep the conversation in my email account for as long as it is useful for helping you, and delete it on request.

Your rights

Because the app collects nothing, I hold no data about you unless you email me or buy Repbook Pro. For data I hold, you can ask for a copy, a correction or deletion, or object to how it is used, by writing to the address below. Sales records that bookkeeping law requires me to keep are deleted when that duty ends. You can also complain to the Norwegian Data Protection Authority, Datatilsynet.

Children

Repbook Iron Plan is not directed at children under 13 and collects no data from anyone.
